## 8. Group-theoretic (`PSL(2,7) = GL(3,2)`, 168 elements)

- **Abstract affine model** (independent of the GL(3,2)/XOR-triple realization already in this catalog — a second, different concrete presentation of the same abstract group, NOT yet checked cycle-for-cycle against the `(1 5 4)(2 3 6)` example above): label the 7 points as `ℤ/7`; F₂₁ = `{x ↦ a·x + b : a ∈ {1,2,4}, b ∈ ℤ/7}`. `{1,2,4}` is exactly the order-3 subgroup of `(ℤ/7)ˣ` (the quadratic residues mod 7, since `(ℤ/7)ˣ` is cyclic of order 6). - **Open, not done:** whether the ℤ/7-affine labeling above and the already-computed GL(3,2)/XOR-triple cycles (`(1 5 4)(2 3 6)`, etc.) are the *same* concrete permutation representation under some relabeling, or two genuinely different embeddings of the abstract group F₂₁ into `Sym(7)`. - **Proposed use, TESTED 2026-09-25 — does not work as a direct replacement.** `VAPMIP/benchmarks/blackjack_vs_horner_bench.py`: (1) composing a *word* of F₂₁ elements is lossy by pigeonhole — only 21 possible final states exist, so any two digit-sequences past a short bound collide (concrete instance: `(0,)` and `(0,0)` both compose to the identity permutation); (2) used the only lossless way — as a size-21 digit alphabet for ordinary Horner (keeping the full sequence, never composing it away) — it needs ~1.8–2× more digits than base-97 for the same text AND is 5–21× slower to encode, 2–8× slower to decode, on the same reference machine as `VAPMIP/README.md`'s baseline. **Verdict: the Blackjack subgroup is not viable as a direct Horner-summing replacement.** See "The Amygdala reading" note in `box_kite.md` for what it might still be good for.
