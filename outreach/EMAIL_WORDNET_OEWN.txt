TITLE

  Collaboration request: a specification for structure above the sentence
  over WordNet relations — what are paragraphs and chapters, as objects
  built from the same relation inventory?

BODY

  This came out of an engineering experiment on the relation graph. I built
  a context-addressing layer that is a pure function of the WordNet relation
  structure: `context_addr(synset)` reads only the 19 relation counts per
  synset (hypernyms, hyponyms, meronyms, and the rest) and never looks at
  the surface word. It maps each synset to a compact fixed-size address — a
  bijective encoding of the count vector, with the counts factored into a
  small number of independent components.

  On the current Open English WordNet build it round-trips bit-exact on
  146,743 synsets: the 19-vector is recovered from the address exactly,
  146,743/146,743 (100.000%), re-verified directly against the live build
  this session. There is one deliberate lossy step — a log-compression of
  the target counts — which affects about 1.1% of individual relation
  slots (not synsets); the rest are exact single-value buckets. Code,
  five executed notebooks, and the round-trip report — the paper is
  titled *19 Dimensional Scalar WordNet Context Propagation*:
  https://github.com/michaelrendier/FourthAgePapers/tree/scalar-context-propagation/ScalarContextPropagation

  The repos behind it, if useful — not required reading for the
  questions below, just the wider context this address sits inside:
    VAPMIP            https://github.com/michaelrendier/VAPMIP
    ValaQuenta        https://github.com/michaelrendier/ValaQuenta
    Ainulindale       https://github.com/michaelrendier/Ainulindale
    FourthAgePapers   https://github.com/michaelrendier/FourthAgePapers

  Since the last draft of this note I also chained this address to a
  word-spelling encoding (a separate, disjoint-prime-tier layer — it never
  touches the relation graph, so it doesn't change anything above): the
  two combine into one prime number per word that recovers both the exact
  spelling and the full 19-vector, verified end to end on live samples.
  Mentioned only so the relation-fingerprint half — the part I'm asking
  about here — is understood as one composable layer in a larger address,
  not the whole of it.

  That gives a clean, comparable object for a word. Composing those objects,
  a sentence is the result of two operators over the word addresses:

    - additive — context accumulation: the component-wise sum of the word
      vectors;
    - multiplicative — grammatical action: a head operates on its dependent.
      Some head/dependent pairs fail here — an agreement or category clash.

  A sentence is complete when every obligatory argument is filled (no
  unfilled slot).

  WHAT I WANT TO DESIGN, AND WOULD LIKE YOUR HELP ON: the specification for
  the levels above the sentence. If a word is a relation fingerprint and a
  sentence is a composition of them, what is a paragraph? a chapter?

  My working sketch (appendix): a paragraph is a chain of sentences linked
  at coreference points (pronoun, definite article, ellipsis) — shared
  relations that cross sentence boundaries; a chapter is a chain of those.
  At each level a stable core persists — the part that is invariant under
  paraphrase of the level below (a paragraph's topic is what survives when
  you paraphrase away sentence-level detail) — and the rest is level-specific
  detail that the next level up summarizes away. Reading runs the hierarchy
  downward (chapter theme -> paragraph topic -> sentence assertion -> word
  sense); writing runs it up.

  That is a sketch, and it is exactly the kind of thing that should be
  shaped by people who have spent years with the relation inventory.

APPENDIX (link or attach) — the hierarchy sketch

  level        object                              complete when
  ---------    ---------------------------------    ----------------------------
  letter       one directional "letter"            —
  word         a relation fingerprint — the 19     the 19-vector is fixed
               WN relation counts as a compact
               fixed-size address
  sentence     a composition of word addresses;    every obligatory argument
               `+` context sum, `x` grammatical    of the predication is
               action (head on dependent)          filled
  paragraph    a chain of sentences joined at      a topic is introduced,
               coreference points (shared          developed, and returned to
               relations across sentence
               boundaries)
  chapter      a chain of paragraphs               setup -> complication ->
                                                   resolution returns the
                                                   setup transformed
  book         a chain of chapters                 —

  THE THROUGH-LINE: a layered structure where each level composes the one
  below and carries roughly twice the state. Between levels there is a
  consistent rule: a fixed core persists (the topic / theme — invariant
  under paraphrase of the level below) and the remaining detail is
  integrated out by the next level. Reading is running that rule down;
  writing is running it up.

  Prior art to align with: Rhetorical Structure Theory (Mann & Thompson) —
  this is the graph, not tree, version; SDRT (Asher & Lascarides); Centering
  Theory (Grosz, Joshi, Weinstein) — the coreference links are the
  backward-looking center; DRT (Kamp). The WN relations are the atoms; the
  spec extends the same relational vocabulary from synset level to discourse
  level.
